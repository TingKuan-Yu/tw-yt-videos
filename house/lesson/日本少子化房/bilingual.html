房價與人口的矛盾 / The Housing and Population Paradox

Pair 1

日本已經走過這個過程：出生率下降不會讓房價全面崩盤，卻會讓這類房子變成沒人要的遺產。
Japan has already gone through this process: the declining birth rate won't cause a complete collapse in housing prices, but it will turn these houses into unwanted legacies.

Pair 2

2026 年 8 月，日本總人口僅有 1 億 2,268 萬人，比前一年同月減少 59 萬人，相當於一年內蒸發了一整座中型城市。
In August 2026, Japan's total population was only 122.68 million, a decrease of 590,000 from the same month of the previous year— equivalent to an entire medium-sized city evaporating in a single year.

Pair 3

2025 年，日本新生兒只有 67 萬 1,000 人，同期空屋數量則正式突破 900 萬戶，空屋率達 13.8%。
In 2025, Japan had only 671,000 newborns, while during the same period, the number of vacant houses in Japan officially exceeded 9 million, with a vacancy rate of 13.8%.

Pair 4

這兩項數字都打破了歷史紀錄。
Both figures broke historical records.

Pair 5

人口持續減少，房屋數量卻持續增加。
The population has been decreasing, while the number of houses has been increasing.

Pair 6

照理說，日本房價早就應該像雪崩一樣崩落了，對吧？
Logically, Japanese housing prices should have collapsed like an avalanche long ago, right?

Pair 7

然而，同一年，東京 23 區新建公寓的平均成交價達到 1 億 3,784 萬日圓，約合新台幣 2,800 萬元，比前一年上漲超過 18%，再次刷新歷史紀錄。
However, in the same year, the average transaction price of newly built apartments in Tokyo's 23 wards reached 137.84 million yen, equivalent to nearly 28 million New Taiwan Dollars, an increase of more than 18% over the previous year, once again breaking the historical record.

Pair 8

2026 年 3 月公布的地價資料顯示，全國所有用途的平均地價已連續五年上漲，而且漲幅仍在擴大。
Land price disclosures released in March 2026 showed that the national average price for all uses had increased for five consecutive years, and the rate of increase was still expanding.

Pair 9

東京都會區住宅用地的變動率達到 5.7%。
The residential land change rate in the Tokyo metropolitan area reached 5.7%.

Pair 10

更引人注目的是，東京新建公寓均價超過 1 億日圓的行政區已增加至 19 區，占比達 80%。
Even more strikingly, the number of wards in Tokyo whose average price for newly built apartments exceeded 100 million yen had expanded to 19, accounting for 80%.

Pair 11

就連過去被視為低收入地區的足立、荒川、板橋與墨田區，也在 206 年首次突破 1 億日圓大關。 [Needs verification]
Even Adachi, Arakawa, Itabashi, and Sumida wards, previously considered low-income areas, broke the 100 million yen mark for the first time in 206. [Needs verification]

Pair 12

千代田區的平均價格更飆升至 3 億 5,150 萬日圓。
The average price in Chiyoda ward soared to 351.5 million yen.

Pair 13

一邊有 900 萬戶無人居住的房屋，另一邊連低收入地區的新公寓也超過 1 億日圓。
On one hand, there are 9 million unoccupied houses, and on the other hand, new apartments in even low-income areas have exceeded 100 million yen.

Pair 14

這兩件事在同一個國家、同一年同時發生。
These two events occurred simultaneously in the same country and in the same year.

Pair 15

所以，出生率下降真的造成房價崩盤了嗎？
So, did the declining birth rate cause a collapse in housing prices?

Pair 16

答案是否定的。
The answer is no.

Pair 17

至少，並不是我們想像的那種崩盤。
At least, it didn't collapse in the way we imagined.

Pair 18

真正崩潰的東西比價格更可怕，而且已在日本悄悄發生了 30 年。
What truly collapsed was more terrifying than prices, and it had been quietly happening in Japan for 30 years.

Pair 19

如今，台灣也已經走到同一條路的半途。
Now, Taiwan is halfway down the same path.

人口、家戶與成本 / Population, Households, and Costs

先讓我們完整攤開日本的情況。
Let's first lay out Japan's situation in its entirety.

日本總人口約在 2008 年達到高峰，之後持續下降，直到 2026 年 3 月。
Japan's total population peaked around 2008 and then declined steadily until March 2026.

15 歲以下的兒童僅有 1,331 萬 9,000 人，一年減少 36 萬人，年減幅為 2.63%。
The number of children under 15 was only 13.319 million, a decrease of 360,000 in one year, a decrease of 2.63% annually.

On one hand, children are disappearing at a rate of over 2% per year, and on the other hand, the super-elderly are accumulating at almost the same rate.

然而，學者只評論說，止跌不代表反彈。
However, scholars only commented that stopping the decline does not mean a rebound.

日本人口下降的整體趨勢完全沒有改變。
The overall trend of Japan's population decline has not changed at all.

This is the underlying reality of Japan, a country that loses 500,000 to 600,000 people every year.

如果房價真的由人口決定，日本的房價曲線應該像瀑布一樣。
If housing prices were truly determined by population, Japan's housing price curve should be a waterfall.

但現實並不是如此。
But reality is not like that.

Why?

The first reason is that the population is decreasing, but the demand for rooftops has not decreased at the same time because households are getting smaller.

Pair 17

單身、晚婚、不婚、離婚與長者獨居，正把相同人口分成更多小單位，而每個單位都需要一個門牌。
Singlehood, late marriage, not marrying, divorce, and elderly people living alone are dividing the same population into more segments, each requiring a house number.

Pair 18

日本家戶數的高峰比人口高峰晚了十多年。
The peak of Japan's household count occurred more than a decade later than the peak of its population.

Pair 19

人口變少，門牌數卻沒有減少。
The population is smaller, but the number of house numbers has not decreased.

Pair 20

第二個原因是，人口並非均勻地消失。
The second reason is that the population is not disappearing evenly.

Pair 21

人們從鄉村移往縣府所在地，從縣府所在地移往都會區，再從都會區移往東京。
People are moving from rural areas to prefectural capitals, from prefectural capitals to metropolitan areas, and from metropolitan areas to Tokyo.

Pair 22

總人口在減少，但東京都會區的人口仍在增加。
The total population is decreasing, but the population of the Tokyo metropolitan area is still increasing.

Pair 23

需求沒有消失，只是轉移了。
The demand has not disappeared; it has simply shifted.

Pair 24

此外，第三個原因也是最容易被忽略的：房價從來不是由人口決定的。
Moreover, the third reason, which is also the most easily overlooked, is that housing prices are never determined by population.

Pair 25

新建住宅的價格由土地成本、建材、工資、利率，以及銀行願意貸出多少錢所決定。
The price of newly built houses is determined by land costs, construction materials, wages, interest rates, and how much money banks are willing to lend.

Pair 26

近年來，日本建築成本飆升、勞動力嚴重短缺，都市地價也上漲了。
In recent years, Japan's construction costs have skyrocketed, labor shortages are severe, and land prices in urban areas have risen.

Pair 27

即使街上沒有買家，新房子的成本價也無法降低。
Even if there are no buyers on the street, the cost price of new houses cannot be reduced.

Pair 28

成本是硬的，需求是軟的，而最終支撐價格的是成本。
Costs are hard, demand is soft, and what ultimately supports prices is cost.

Pair 29

第四個原因是，全國平均值是一個具有誤導性的數字。
The fourth reason is that the national average is a deceptive figure.

Pair 30

當東京上漲 20%、其他地區下跌 10% 時，平均值看起來只是溫和上漲，你可能會以為市場很健康。
When Tokyo rises by 20% and other regions fall by 10%, the average appears to be a moderate increase, and you might think the market is healthy.

Pair 31

But beneath that average lies millions of houses that have ceased to exist.

Pair 32

指數不會宣告死者的死亡。
The index will not announce the death of the dead.

空屋分類與市場分化 / Vacancy Categories and Market Polarization

Pair 1

此外，「空屋」這個詞本身也具有誤導性。
Moreover, the term "empty house" itself is deceptive.

Pair 2

日本 900 萬戶空屋中，有很大一部分是待租、待售，或別墅之類的二手住宅。
Of Japan's 9 million empty houses, a large portion are for rent, for sale, or second-hand residences such as villas.

Pair 3

至少它們還在市場上流通。
At least they are still circulating in the market.

Pair 4

真正致命的是另一類，日本人稱為「其他空屋」：不出售、不出租、也不使用，只是放在那裡的房屋。
The truly fatal one is another category, which the Japanese call "other empty houses"—houses that are not for sale, not for rent, and are not used; they are simply left there.

Pair 5

2023 年，這個數量是 385 萬戶，占所有住宅的 5%。
In 2023, this number was 3.85 million, accounting for 5% of all housing units.

The vacancy rate in 2023 was 9%, a 2.5-fold increase compared to 1993. [Needs verification]

More notably, the total number of vacant houses in 2023 increased by 510,000 compared to 2018, and about 70% of these were completely abandoned vacant houses.

Pair 8

還有一個常見誤解，認為空屋是鄉村才有的現象。
There's also a common misconception that vacant houses are a rural phenomenon.

Pair 9

這是不對的。
This is incorrect.

In terms of the number of households, Osaka Prefecture had the most vacant houses with 227,000, followed by Tokyo with 215,000, and Hyogo Prefecture with 173,000 – all located in major metropolitan areas.

Pair 11

因為分母很大，空屋率可能看似偏低，但絕對數量卻是全國最驚人的。
The vacancy rate may appear low due to the large denominator, but the absolute number is the most dramatic nationwide.

Pair 12

空屋並非先從鄉村開始，而是在每座城市的外圍同時出現。
The vacancy rate didn't start in the countryside; it began simultaneously on the outskirts of every city.

Pair 13

事實上，2026 年日本地價報告揭露了一個轉折：札幌、仙台、廣島與福岡這四座地方城市，十多年來持續優於三大都會區，卻出現顯著的成長放緩，14 年來首次落後於三大都會區。
In fact, the 2026 Japan Land Price Report revealed a turning point: the four regional cities of Sapporo, Sendai, Hiroshima, and Fukuoka, which had consistently outperformed the three major metropolitan areas for over a decade, saw a significant slowdown in growth, losing to the three major metropolitan areas for the first time in 14 years.

Pair 14

連地方表現最好的城市都開始吃力，這才是兩極分化的真實樣貌。
Even the top performers in the region are starting to struggle – this is the true picture of polarization.

Pair 15

不只是東京上漲、鄉村下跌那麼簡單，連中間階層也開始落後。
It's not as simple as Tokyo rising and rural areas falling; even the middle classes are starting to fall behind.

流動性與持有負擔 / Liquidity and Ownership Burdens

Pair 1

所以，日本究竟崩潰了什麼？
So, what exactly collapsed in Japan?

Pair 2

答案是流動性。
The answer is liquidity.

Pair 3

我們習慣以價格來思考資產。
We're used to thinking about assets in terms of price.

Pair 4

如果一間房子從 1,000 萬跌到 500 萬，我們會說它跌了一半，雖然很痛，但至少還有價值。
If a house drops from 10 million to 5 million, we say it's fallen by half, which hurts, but at least it still has value.

Pair 5

日本教會我們，還有一種更徹底的死亡方式：不管開價多少，都沒有人願意接手。
What Japan taught us is that there's an even more thorough way to die: no matter the asking price, no one is willing to take it.

Pair 6

價格不是慢慢下跌，而是有一天因為市場上再也沒有出價者而消失。
The price doesn't just slowly decline; it disappears one day because there are no more bidders in the market.

Pair 7

日本人給它一個殘酷的名稱：「負的動產」。 [Needs verification]
The Japanese have a cruel name for this: negative movable property. [Needs verification]

Pair 8

它不是不動產，而是「負的動產」。 [Needs verification]
It's not real estate, it's negative movable property. [Needs verification]

Pair 9

It won't give you rent or appreciation, but it will send you bills on time every year: fixed asset tax, city planning tax, management fees, repair fund, weeding, snow removal, fire insurance.

Pair 10

即使想送人，也沒有人敢接手，因為在日本，房屋一旦轉到你名下，你就要對整棟建築的共同債務負連帶責任。
If you want to give it away, no one dares to take it because in Japan, once a house is transferred to your name, you are jointly liable for the common debts of the entire building.

How much management fees the previous owners owed is now your debt.

Pair 12

於是，經濟史上罕見的一幕出現了。
Thus, a scene rarely seen in economic history has emerged.

Pair 13

當一項商品的價格從零變成負數，你不一定是要賣掉它，而是必須付錢請人接手。
If the price of a product goes from zero to a negative number, you don't necessarily have to sell it; you have to pay someone to take it.

Pair 14

這聽起來很抽象，所以讓我們到現場看看。
This sounds abstract, so let's go to the scene.

2025 年，全台新建案總銷售額約為新台幣 2.1 兆元，年減 11.2%。
In 2025, the total sales of new housing projects across Taiwan were approximately NT$2.1 trillion, a decrease of 11.2% year-on-year.

Pair 4

市場普遍預測，2026 年房價將修正 5% 到 10%。
The market generally predicts that housing prices will see a correction of 5% to 10% in 2026.

Pair 5

台北因基期高且有剛性需求支撐，預期修正幅度約為 5% 到 6%。
In Taipei, due to a high base and rigid demand supporting the market, the correction is expected to be around 5% to 6%.

Pair 6

中南部部分地區因前兩年漲幅過大、投資比例高，修正幅度可能超過 10%。
In some areas of central and southern Taiwan, due to excessive price increases in the previous two years and a high proportion of investment, the correction may exceed 10%.

Pair 7

2026 年 3 月 20 日，中央銀行小幅放寬自然人第二戶住宅的貸款成數，從 50% 調高至 60%。
On March 20, 2026, the Central Bank slightly relaxed the loan-to-value ratio for second homes for individuals, raising it from 50% to 60%.

Pair 8

然而，整體管制架構維持不變。
However, the overall regulatory framework remains unchanged.

Pair 9

真正的變數在供給端。
The real variable lies on the supply side.

Pair 10

大量在 2024 到 2025 年取得建照的預售建案，將於 2026 到 2027 年完工交屋，預估新增供給為 32 萬到 36 萬戶。
A large number of pre-sale projects that obtained building permits from 2024 to 2025 will be completed and delivered in 2026 to 2027, with an estimated new supply of 320,000 to 360,000 units.

Pair 11

當這批新屋湧入買氣尚未恢復的市場時，最大的壓力會落在哪裡？
When this batch of new homes floods into a market where buying interest has not yet recovered, where will the greatest pressure come from?

Pair 12

不會是台北市中心，而是新建案高度集中、生活機能尚未成熟的那些重劃地區。
It won't be downtown Taipei, but rather those redevelopment areas with a high concentration of new housing developments and underdeveloped living amenities.

Pair 13

同時，政府已開始做日本正在做的事：內政部推出總預算新台幣 50 億元的老屋延壽計畫，提供 30 年以上住宅修繕補助，最高實際給付 65%，金額可達新台幣 960 萬元。
Meanwhile, the government has already begun doing what Japan is doing: the Ministry of the Interior has launched an old building life extension program with a total budget of NT$5 billion, providing repair subsidies for residences over 30 years old, with a maximum actual payment of 65%, which can amount to NT$9.6 million.

Pair 14

此外，規定必須先修繕外牆、屋頂與電梯，才能申請室內補助。
Moreover, it stipulates that the exterior walls, roofs, and elevators must be repaired first before applying for indoor subsidies.

Pair 15

計畫將執行到 2027 年底。
The program will be implemented until the end of 2027.

Pair 16

這項政策的弦外之音很清楚：台灣超過 500 萬間老屋，不可能全部拆除重建。
The subtext of this policy is actually very clear: it is impossible to demolish and rebuild all of the more than 5 million old houses in Taiwan.

Pair 17

台灣都市更新與危老重建的速度有多慢？
How slow is the pace of urban renewal and dilapidated housing renovation in Taiwan?

Pair 18

2023 年，全台只核准了 327 件都市更新案，以及 1,874 件危險及老舊建築重建案。
In 2023, only 327 urban renewal projects were approved across Taiwan, while 1,874 projects were approved for reconstruction of dilapidated and old buildings.

Pair 19

危老重建的門檻是全體屋主都必須同意。
The threshold for reconstruction of dilapidated and old buildings is that all owners must agree.

Pair 20

只要有一戶不同意，整個計畫就會被卡住。
If even one household disagrees, the entire project is blocked.

Pair 21

比法律更困難的是人心。
What's more difficult is not the law, but people's hearts.

Pair 22

實務上，最常見的情況是，有些長者在這棟樓住了 40 年，不願看它被拆掉。
In practice, the most common situation is that some elderly people have lived in this building for 40 years and are reluctant to see it demolished.

Pair 23

有人擔心過程太久，自己等不到新建築完工。
Some people worry that the process will take too long and they will not be able to wait for the new building to be completed.

Pair 24

有人覺得搬家很麻煩，寧可繼續忍受漏水。
Some people find moving troublesome and would rather continue to endure the leaks.

Pair 25

最無奈的是，經濟條件較好的屋主早已搬到新樓，只把老公寓拿來收租。
And the most helpless situation is that homeowners with better economic conditions have already moved to new buildings and only use the old apartment to collect rent.

Pair 26

他們不必承受爬樓梯的辛苦與外牆剝落的困擾，自然沒有迫切的重建需求。
They do not feel the pain of climbing difficult stairs and peeling exterior walls, and naturally have no urgency for reconstruction.

Pair 27

結果，最需要重建的居民，被最不需要重建的屋主鎖在原地。
As a result, the residents who most need reconstruction are locked in place by the homeowners who least need reconstruction.

Pair 28

換句話說，台灣和日本一樣，已默默做出選擇：少數房屋會重建，大多數只能延長壽命，另有一些將悄悄走完生命週期。
In other words, Taiwan, like Japan, has silently made a choice: a few houses will be rebuilt, most houses can only have their lifespan extended, and some will silently complete their life cycle.

風險特徵與三個問題 / Risk Characteristics and Three Questions

Pair 1

把日本 30 年的經驗濃縮起來，我們就能拼出一幅很清楚的圖像。
Well, by compressing Japan's 30 years of experience, we can piece together a very clear picture.

Pair 2

在出生率下降的社會中，什麼樣的房屋會變成沒人要的遺產？
What kind of houses become unwanted legacies in a society with a declining birth rate?

Pair 3

第一，是位於極遠郊、只能開車抵達、沒有軌道交通，而且通勤超過一個半小時的房屋。
First, those located in super suburbs accessible only by car, with no rail transport and commutes exceeding one and a half hours.

Pair 4

第二，是屋齡超過金融機構願意承作長期貸款年限，讓下一位買家無法取得房貸的房屋。
Second, those older than the years that financial institutions are willing to undertake long-term loans for, making it impossible for the next buyer to obtain a mortgage.

Pair 5

第三，是採區分所有權，卻管理失靈、修繕基金長期不足，甚至無法召開區分所有權人會議的房屋。
Third, those belonging to separate ownership, but with dysfunctional management, chronically insufficient maintenance funds, and even the inability to hold separate owners' meetings.

Pair 6

第四，是沒有電梯的中低樓層公寓，而且居民平均年齡遠高於社會平均。
Fourth, those mid- to low-rise apartments without elevators, where the average age of residents is far higher than the social average.

Pair 7

第五，是功能單一的房屋。
Fifth, those with only a single function.

Pair 8

第六，是只供睡覺的住宅社區、溫泉度假住宅，或純為炒作而買的重劃區小宅；第七，是面積與格局脫離現今家庭結構，無法分割或改造，而且所在地缺乏產業、工作機會與新增人口流入的房屋。 [Needs verification]
Sixth, a residential community solely for sleeping, a resort-style hot spring residence, or a small house in a redevelopment zone purely for speculation; seventh, the size and layout are out of touch with current family structures, making it impossible to subdivide or renovate; and the area lacks industry, job opportunities, and new population inflow. [Needs verification]

Pair 9

這七點符合得越多，你的房產就越接近變成負債。
The more of these seven points you encounter, the closer your property is to becoming a liability.

Pair 10

請特別注意：這些項目沒有一項是在說房價下跌，而是全部指向同一件事，未來某一天，你會找不到人接手。
Please pay special attention: none of these items indicate a drop in property prices; they all point to the same thing: one day in the future, you won't find anyone to take over.

Pair 11

所以，如果你現在正在看房、繳貸款，或煩惱父母老公寓該怎麼辦，根據日本 30 年的經驗，你只剩三個問題：第一，20 年後這個地方還會有人嗎？
So, if you are currently looking at houses, paying off a loan, or worrying about what to do with your parents' old apartment, based on 30 years of experience in Japan, you're left with only three questions: First, will there still be people in this place in 20 years?

Pair 12

看的不是現在有多少人，而是是否會有新人願意搬進來、是否有工作機會、是否有軌道交通，以及是否有理由讓年輕家庭留下。
It's not about how many people are there now, but whether new people will be willing to move in, whether there are job opportunities, whether there is rail transportation, and whether there are reasons for young families to stay.

Pair 13

一個只靠人口慣性維持的地方，也會隨著那股慣性停下來。
A place that lives solely on population inertia will stop along with that inertia.

Pair 14

第二，20 年後還有人管理這棟建築嗎？
Second, will anyone still be managing this building in 20 years?

Pair 15

管理委員會會正常運作嗎？
Will the management committee be operating normally?

Pair 16

公共基金收得到嗎？
Will public funds be collected?

Pair 17

如果十年後建築需要整修、更換電梯與修繕管線，居民能籌到錢嗎？
If, ten years from now, the building needs renovations, elevator replacements, and utility repairs, will the residents be able to raise the funds?

Pair 18

在出生率下降的社會中，管理比屋齡更重要，因為屋齡只會朝一個方向增加，管理卻決定它是優雅地老去，還是難看地老去。
In a society with a declining birth rate, management is more important than the building's age because building age only goes in one direction, while management determines whether it ages gracefully or unsightly.

Pair 19

第三個問題是，20 年後，下一位買家還借得到錢嗎？
The third question is, 20 years from now, will the next buyer be able to borrow money?

Pair 20

這是最容易被忽略，卻也最致命的問題。
This is the most easily overlooked yet most fatal question.

Pair 21

房子的價值從來不是你認為它值多少，而是下一個人能否在合理條件下買下它。
The value of a house is never how much you think it's worth, but whether the next person can buy it under reasonable conditions.

Pair 22

當銀行不願放貸，市場上就沒有買家；沒有買家，紙面價格就只是一個數字。
When banks are unwilling to lend, there are no buyers in the market, and without buyers, the price on paper is just a number.

Pair 23

日本的教訓不是房價會崩盤，而是流動性會死亡。
Japan's lesson isn't that housing prices will collapse, but that liquidity will die.

Pair 24

而流動性由制度、地段與管理三者共同決定。
And liquidity is determined by three things together: the system, location, and management.

分化、預測與世代負擔 / Polarization, Forecasts, and Generational Burdens

Pair 1

回到最初的矛盾：出生率下降會讓房價全面崩盤嗎？
Let's return to the initial contradiction: Will declining birth rates cause a complete collapse in housing prices?

Pair 2

日本的實際經驗顯示，答案是否定的。
Empirical evidence from Japan suggests the answer is no.

Pair 3

全國指數不會崩盤，都市核心也不會。
The national index won't collapse, nor will urban centers.

Pair 4

有產業、交通與有效管理的地區不只會保持穩定，甚至可能因需求集中而變得更貴。
Areas with industry, transportation, and effective management will not only remain stable but may even become more expensive due to concentrated demand.

Pair 5

這解釋了為什麼一個每年少 60 萬人的國家，同時能擁有全球最昂貴的部分都市住宅。
This explains why a country with 600,000 fewer people per year can simultaneously possess some of the world's most expensive urban housing prices.

Pair 6

然而，同樣的出生率下降，會非常安靜且明確地把另一批房產從資產清單中移除。
However, this same declining birth rate will very quietly and definitively remove another group of properties from the asset list.

Pair 7

它們不會登上新聞頭條，因為它們沒有成交紀錄。
They won't make headlines because they have no sales records.

Pair 8

沒有成交紀錄，就沒有房價下跌的新聞。
Without sales records, there's no news of price drops.

Pair 9

它們只會落在某個中年人的抽屜裡，變成每年的稅單、無止盡的管理費、永遠開不完的屋主會議，以及父母辛苦留下卻沒人願意接手的心血。
They'll just end up in some middle-aged person's drawer, becoming an annual tax bill, an endless management fee, a never-ending meeting of property owners, and a piece of parents' hard work that no one wants to take over.

Pair 10

日本花了 30 年走到 900 萬戶空屋的局面，而民間機構估計，到 2043 年，空屋可能達到 1,861 萬戶，空屋率為 25.3%，也就是每四間房就有一間空屋。
Japan took 30 years to get 9 million houses to this point, and private organizations estimate that by 2043, the number of vacant houses in Japan may reach 18.61 million, with a vacancy rate of 25.3%, meaning that one in four houses will be vacant.

Pair 11

另一項估計更直接：到 2038 年，日本每三間房就會有一間空屋。
Another estimate is even more direct: by 2038, one in three houses in Japan will be vacant.

Pair 12

那台灣呢？
What about Taiwan?

住宅平均屋齡已經 32 年，老屋數量每年增加超過 20 萬戶。
The average age of residential buildings is already 32 years, and the number of old houses is increasing by more than 200,000 every year.

Pair 14

空屋率剛創下 15 年新高。
The vacancy rate has just hit a 15-year high.

20 坪以下小宅（約 33 平方公尺）的空屋率顯著偏高，而人口已進入負成長。 [Needs verification]
The vacancy rate of small houses under 20 ping (approximately 33 square meters) is significantly high, while the population has entered negative growth. [Needs verification]

Pair 16

我們不是還在起點，只是尚未走到最痛苦的階段。
We are not yet at the beginning; we just haven't reached the most painful stage yet.

有人可能會說：「那我就只買市中心。」
Some people might say, "Then I'll just buy in the city center."

Pair 18

這沒錯，卻其實避開了一個更令人清醒的事實。
That's true, but it actually avoids a more sobering fact.

Pair 19

日本這場變化真正殘酷的地方，不是有些房屋價格下跌，而是它重新分配了整個社會的居住成本。
The real cruelty of this change in Japan is not that some houses have fallen in price, but that it has redistributed the cost of housing for the entire society.

年輕人買不起市中心，只好搬出去。
Young people can't afford to buy in the city center, so they have to move out.

搬出去後，他們買到的是未來會失去流動性的房屋。
When they move out, they buy houses that will lose liquidity in the future.

When they get old and want to exchange their houses for retirement funds, they find that they can't sell their properties at all.

真正安全的地區，在他們年輕時就已經買不起。
The truly safe areas were already unaffordable when they were young.

這就是出生率下降最現實的圖像。
This is the most realistic picture of the declining birthrate.

它不是所有人共同承受的崩盤，而是一場無聲的分流。
It's not a collapse that everyone shares, but a silent diversion.

Some are diverted to the side with an exit, while others are diverted to the side with no exit.

And the gate of diversion is often closed the day you sign that sales contract.

So, I'll leave you with a question.

If the value of a house ultimately depends on whether anyone is willing to buy it 20 years from now, then what we're desperately buying now—is it an asset or a subscription that the next generation has to pay for on our behalf?

如果今天就把你擁有的房子交給孩子，你覺得他會說謝謝，還是沉默很久？
